The Mad Scientist Class

Some people simply cannot leave anything alone.

BMW has given us some extraordinary things over the years. Brilliant engines, beautiful chassis and some of the greatest combinations of the two. But sometimes BMW gave us the right engine in the wrong car. Sometimes they gave us a gorgeous chassis that makes you wonder whether another BMW powerplant would have been better between those Bavarian fenders.

And sometimes you look at that beautiful BMW and think:

“You know what would fit perfectly in there? An engine BMW didn't even make.”

Welcome to Werksport, the engine swap class of Bavarian Endurance Battle. The Frankenstein experiment happens under the hood. The BMW chassis remains the foundation.

This isn't about tube chassis prototypes or completely reinventing the car. It's about taking a BMW and giving it the heart you believe it should have had.

Now, About The Person Responsible For This...

There is a particular kind of gearhead.

There is a particular kind of gearhead who simply cannot leave anything alone. These are the fabricators, tuners, engine builders and dangerously curious people who actually understand how this shit works.

Or at least they're extremely confident that they do.

Mention the word “stock” to one of these guys and you'll probably watch him gag before quietly walking away from you. To the Werksport mind, leaving something exactly the way BMW built it isn't preservation. It's a missed opportunity.

Ask him about his BMW at a party and twenty minutes later some poor girl is holding a drink while receiving an unsolicited explanation of compression ratios, injector duty cycles, weight transfer and why his particular engine combination is obviously superior.

She stopped listening six parties ago. He still hasn't noticed.

This is the guy who has a welder for a nightstand. There is no wall separating his bedroom from the engine build room because, according to him, “the wall was just unnecessary weight.”

His kitchen table has connecting rods on it, his laptop is datalogging instead of playing Netflix, and somewhere in the house there is probably an engine waiting for a car he hasn't bought yet.

Underneath all the insanity, however, is something we genuinely respect. These are builders, fabricators and problem solvers. When the part they need doesn't exist, they make it. When two things were never designed to work together, they figure out how to introduce them.

This guy actually knows how shit works.

Or at least he REALLY thinks he does.

Twelve hours is going to tell us which one.
